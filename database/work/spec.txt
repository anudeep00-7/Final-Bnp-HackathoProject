I. Use Case Short Title 
Corporate Actions Processing and Portfolio Impact Hub 
II. Problem Statement & Background 
Corporate actions are events initiated by a listed company that bring material change to its securities—and 
directly affect the value, quantity and cash flow of every portfolio holding that security. Banks and custodians 
run dedicated Corporate Actions processing teams because a missed or wrongly calculated action can cause 
direct profit-and-loss impact, client complaints and regulatory breaches, such as failing to pay a dividend or 
adjust a position correctly. 
Asset management firms handle thousands of securities across multiple portfolios. When companies 
announce actions such as dividends, bonus issues, stock splits and rights issues, information may sit across 
spreadsheets, emails and separate data files. This creates manual tracking, missed deadlines, limited impact 
visibility, compliance gaps and siloed views. 
• Business challenge: Track corporate-action events across all portfolios, calculate the total cash or 
security entitlement, ensure settlement is reflected by the payment or effective date, and document 
every processing decision for compliance. 
• User need: The Admin needs visibility of holdings, upcoming actions, eligibility and portfolio impact 
on different dates. The Analyst needs a clear view of how events affect portfolio holdings, cash and 
value over different periods. 
• Expected flow: Import data, review events, determine eligibility, preview impact, process an action, 
update quantity, cost basis, cash and market value, reconcile the result and display an audit trail. 
 
III. Objective  
Design and build a Corporate Actions Processing and Portfolio Impact Hub that: 
• Captures or ingests corporate-action events for the supplied universe of securities. 
• Maintains a portfolio book containing positions by investor or portfolio. 
• Applies the applicable business rule to adjust quantity, cost basis, cash and market value. 
• Produces cash and security settlement forecasts by portfolio over different periods. 
• Produces a comparison of portfolio value and distribution between two dates, considering & 
explaining the impact of corporate actions. 
• Provides an auditable and explainable trail of every adjustment made. 
• Supports two roles: Admins (s) (view portfolios for all investors) and Analysts (view portfolio(s) 
assigned to them), with role-appropriate access and views. 
• Generate Analyst reports (pdf) detailing portfolio cost, value, past and future actions 
• Apply basic AI/ML on top of the core engine, for

V. Expected Outcomes 
1. Correct portfolio processing: eligible portfolios receive the correct cash or security entitlement for 
the supported actions. 
2. Portfolio impact visibility: users can compare holdings, cash, cost basis, market value and 
distribution before and after processing and between selected dates. 
3. Cash and security reporting: users can view the resulting cash and security movements for each 
portfolio over different periods. 
4. Auditability and reconciliation: every adjustment and decision is traceable, and portfolio valuation 
reconciles before and after applicable value-neutral actions. 
5. Operational usability: pending, processed, failed and reversed events can be identified and explained 
through a unified view. 
6. Practical engineering: the prototype is modular, testable and demonstrable within the 22-hour 
constraint. 
VI. Solution Requirements (Good to Have) 
Functional Requirements 
• Provide simple login or role selection for two user types: Admin and Analyst. 
• Provide a portfolio holdings view, corporate-action calendar or feed, impact preview, processing 
status, reconciliation summary and audit-history view. 
• Allow the Admin to inspect an event, view affected portfolios and process an eligible action. 
• Allow the Analyst to view the relevant portfolio, cash and security position, and the impact of 
processed actions. 
• Apply each event once only and retain complete processing history. 
• Produce cash and security reports by portfolio and a comparison of portfolio value and distribution 
between two dates.

General Business Rules 
• Model announcement date, ex-date, record date and payment or settlement date where supplied. 
• Determine entitlement from the eligible position using the date convention and calculation rules in 
the separate business-rules workbook. 
• Use consistent security, event and portfolio identifiers to join corporate actions, holdings, prices and 
users. 
• Do not process rejected, incomplete, duplicate, cancelled or already processed events. 
• Preserve calculation precision and apply the rounding convention defined in the business-rules 
workbook. 
• Every adjustment must produce an audit record containing action ID, security, portfolio, processing 
date, rule applied, before-and-after quantity, before-and-after cost basis and cash movement. 
• Portfolio valuation—quantity multiplied by price, plus cash—must reconcile before and after 
applicable value-neutral mandatory actions, other than defined leakage or rounding. 
• A reversal must roll back the exact adjustment made by the original action and preserve a linked 
record of both transactions. 
• The detailed business rules are provided separately and take precedence over assumptions made by 
the team.

IX. Dataset 
 
i. `securities.csv` — security master (reference/static data) 
ii. `portfolios.csv` — client/portfolio master (reference/static data) 
iii. `positions.csv` — opening holdings as of 2026-01-01 (bootstrap transactional data) 
iv. `cash_balances.csv` — opening cash per portfolio as of 2026-01-01 
v. `prices.csv` — price marks needed to compute before/after market value at each action date 
vi. `corporate_actions.csv` — the event calendar (mandatory + voluntary), spanning Jan–May 2026 
vii. `ca_elections.csv` — client elections against the voluntary actions in step 6 
viii. `ca_notices_raw.csv` — raw-text announcements paired with ground-truth extraction fields; not 
loaded into the transactional database, used to train/test the NLP extraction and classification 
components
